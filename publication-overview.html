The Vertical Premium

Relationship between relative height of properties from real flood elevation and housing prices

Kim, Y., Koo, B. W., & Lee, S. • Cities, 165, 106126 • 2025
Read Full Paper View on Publisher Site
💡

Key Finding

Properties located higher above flood elevation command significant price premiums of approximately 2-3% per meter, even after controlling for other factors. This effect amplified dramatically after the 2013 Calgary flood.

01

Research Question

How does the relative height of a property from flood elevation affect housing prices?

This study examines the "vertical premium" - the additional value homebuyers place on properties situated higher above potential flood levels.

Why It Matters

  • Flood risk is a growing concern in urban areas
  • Traditional flood zone maps use binary classifications
  • Actual elevation differences are continuous, not binary
  • Understanding vertical premiums can inform better planning

Key Results

Vertical Premium Effect

1

Vertical Premium Exists

2

Post-Flood Amplification

The premium increased significantly after the 2013 flood event

3

Spatial Variation

Effects are strongest in areas with recent flood experience

Innovation

Beyond Binary Flood Zones

This research introduces the concept of "vertical premium" as a distinct component of housing value, separate from traditional flood plain designations. Unlike binary flood zone classifications (in/out), it captures the continuous relationship between elevation and value.
